### Claims and numbers

| Question | MacKenzie's choice | Her note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Twice as much or twice as fast | "Twice as much", a universal rule. Never "twice as fast". | |
| Learning growth figure for general marketing | "2x", the simple general claim with no qualifier. Rejected: the 2.6x Austin K-8 MAP figure and per-context figures. | |

### Punctuation

| Question | MacKenzie's choice | Her note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exclamation marks in headlines and ending cards | Periods in formal headlines; an exclamation mark is acceptable in celebratory social content. | Either periods or no punctuation in formal headlines. |
| Oxford comma | Always use the Oxford comma; it is required in all Alpha copy. | |

## How this applies to the website

- Website headings, buttons, and page copy are formal: no exclamation marks, a period or no punctuation. The celebratory exception is for social posts, not alpha.school pages.
- No em dashes anywhere (the video team's rule, in the marketing workspace rules). En dashes stay only inside ranges such as 40–60%.
- Write team bios, campus copy, FAQs, and launch copy with these terms: AlphaTest, WonderLab, LearningLab, Shadow Day, Alphas, Q Breaks, Mobile Squad (never described as a field trip), life skills workshops, 2 Hour Learning.
- Claims follow the claims rows exactly; a new statistic or a stronger restatement goes to MacKenzie, not to the page.
- The four items held for MacKenzie's decision in `site-rules.md` stay held. The Oxford comma choice above is her rule; what is held is the bulk edit of the probable missing commas on live pages, which needs a human read.
